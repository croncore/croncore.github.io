Capabilities

The integrations companies actually hire us for.

Nobody wakes up wanting an API. They want the quote in the CRM to match the invoice in the accounting system without anyone retyping it. These are the integration problems we solve most often, and the jobs behind them.

CRM and Sales Stack Integration

Salesforce, HubSpot, and Pipedrive are connected to your product, your billing, and your support desk, so a deal closed in the CRM triggers provisioning, invoicing, and onboarding without a human copying fields between tabs. When the work goes beyond connecting and into customizing the platform itself, our Salesforce and ServiceNow team picks it up.

ERP, Accounting and Finance Integration

SAP, NetSuite, QuickBooks, and Xero wired into your operational systems with two way sync, reconciliation rules, and audit trails. Finance integrations are where "mostly accurate" is not good enough, so every sync we build is idempotent and verifiable. If you are still selecting or rolling out the ERP itself, start with our ERP implementation service.

Ecommerce and Payment Integrations

Shopify, WooCommerce, Stripe, and payment gateways connected to inventory, fulfillment, and accounting. Orders flow one way, stock levels flow back, and refunds do not turn into support tickets. Signed webhooks and retry logic mean a checkout spike does not become a data loss incident.

Partner and Public APIs

When your data or functionality becomes a product, we build the API your partners consume: versioned endpoints, OAuth2, rate limiting, usage metering for billing, and a developer portal with docs that reduce your support load instead of adding to it. This is the layer that turns a web or SaaS platform into an ecosystem.

Legacy and EDI Integration

Mainframes, AS/400 systems, on premise databases, and EDI trading partners that predate the modern web. We wrap them in clean modern APIs so the rest of your stack can talk to them, and when the time comes to retire them, that same API layer makes the migration dramatically less painful.

AI Ready APIs and MCP Servers

AI agents are becoming API consumers, and most company APIs are not ready for them. We build MCP servers and agent friendly endpoints with the permissions, guardrails, and structured responses that let AI agents safely read from and act on your systems. If your 2026 roadmap includes agents, your API layer is where that work starts.

One Integration Layer, Not Point to Point Spaghetti.

Ten systems connected point to point means up to 45 separate connections, each one a place data can silently drift. We design around a central integration layer with event driven messaging, so each system connects once and every consumer stays consistent. Fewer connections, fewer failure points, and adding system eleven takes days instead of months.

If It Is Not Monitored, It Is Already Broken.

The most expensive integration failure is the silent one, discovered three weeks later in a financial report. Every integration we ship includes health dashboards, failure alerting, and dead letter queues, backed by our monitoring and observability practice, so you hear about a problem from an alert, not from a customer.

Straight Answers

Cost, timelines, and the maintenance bill nobody mentions.

Integration quotes vary wildly because most of them hide the real scope. Here is what this work actually costs in 2026 and where the budget really goes.

A simple one way sync with a modern, well documented API runs $2,000 to $5,000. A standard two way integration, like CRM to billing, runs $8,000 to $25,000 depending on data volume and edge cases. Legacy system and compliance heavy integrations run $30,000 to $80,000 and up. Building a custom API from scratch for your own product typically starts around $15,000. The API call itself is never the expensive part. Authentication, error handling, data mapping, and testing are where the hours actually go, which is why quotes that only count endpoints always overrun.

Sometimes, yes. iPaaS tools are the right call for light workflows, low volume, and standard apps, and we will tell you when they are enough. Custom integration wins when volume grows past iPaaS pricing tiers, when you need two way sync with conflict resolution, when a legacy or on premise system is involved, or when sensitive data cannot route through a third party. The pattern we see constantly: teams start on Zapier at $60 a month, scale to $1,500 a month, then hire us to build the integration properly and cut the recurring bill. If that is your trajectory, skipping to custom earlier is usually cheaper.

A single straightforward integration ships in 1 to 3 weeks. A multi system integration with two way sync takes 4 to 8 weeks. A full integration layer connecting five or more systems, or a partner facing API product, runs 2 to 4 months. Undocumented legacy systems are the wildcard, which is why our first step is always mapping the systems before we commit to a date.

Here is the number most vendors leave out: plan for roughly 10 to 20 percent of each integration's build cost per year. Vendor APIs change and deprecate versions, usage based fees grow with your volume, rate limits get hit, and someone has to respond when a sync fails at 2am. We cover this with a maintenance agreement that includes monitoring, alerting, and version upgrades, priced per integration so you can see exactly what each connection costs to own, not just to build.
